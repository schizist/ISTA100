Timeline: From Vacuum Tubes to Transistors

1877

Edison's Carbon Button

Thomas Edison discovers that compressed carbon can dramatically improve telephone voice transmission quality, making the telephone truly marketable.

1910

The Oil-Drop Experiment

Robert Millikan's famous experiment provides direct proof of the electron's existence and establishes the foundation for modern physics research.

1915

Transcontinental Telephone Line

The first coast-to-coast telephone line connects New York to San Francisco using vacuum tube repeaters, with Alexander Graham Bell making the ceremonial first call.

1925

Bell Labs Established

AT&T creates Bell Telephone Laboratories as a stand-alone research organization with a budget of $12 million (equivalent to $150 million today), housing 2,000 technical experts.

1930s

The Great Depression Era

Despite economic hardship, Bell Labs continues hiring top talent from universities, offering $3,000 annual salaries when academic positions paid only half that amount.

1947

The Transistor Revolution

On December 16, John Bardeen and Walter Brattain demonstrate the first working transistor at Bell Labs, launching the Information Age and eventually earning the Nobel Prize.

1950s

Silicon Valley Born

William Shockley founds Shockley Transistor Corp. near Stanford University. His team later splits off to form new companies, creating Silicon Valley.
