01 — Introduction

Who I Am and Where I'm Headed

My name is Daniel. I am an electrical engineering student at the University of Arizona, working my way toward a career that sits at the crossroads of mechanical ingenuity, electronics, and creative vision.

During the day I work in the field of cathodic protection. In the evenings and on weekends, I run a home lab where I build things: a pen plotter I designed from scratch, a 3D printer running custom firmware, a smart home system with computer vision cameras, and a growing catalog of CAD models and animations. These are not just hobbies. They are the proving ground for the kind of engineer and builder I intend to be.

This presentation maps my goals, my path, and the future I am designing for myself. It covers where I am now, what I want to build over the next decade, and the kind of impact I intend to have on the people who experience my work.

02 — Designing for My Own Future

Iteration Is How I Think

ISTA 100 introduced design thinking as a formal framework. What it showed me is that I had been doing it instinctively for years. Building a pen plotter means defining the problem (how do I convert an SVG into physical marks on paper?), ideating a solution, prototyping in CAD, testing with real hardware, and iterating when the first approach fails. That cycle never ends. It is just the job.

The lesson I am taking forward is that the same process applies to designing a life. You identify what you want, you prototype a path toward it, and you revise when reality pushes back. Dreams are not fixed destinations. They are living designs that get better with every iteration.

03 — Define My Career Path

Motion Systems. Animatronics. My Studio.

My dream is to design and build motion systems and animatronics for entertainment applications. That means elaborate robotic creatures for film and haunted attractions, fully articulated props with embedded electronics, and 6-degree-of-freedom motion platforms for simulation rigs used in sim racing and flight training.

More importantly, I want to own the full pipeline: from the first rough sketch on paper or a tablet, through engineering and electronics, to a finished machine that moves with presence and character. The end goal is to run my own studio, taking custom commissions and building things that leave people genuinely surprised.

This is not a vague aspiration. It is the throughline that connects everything I already do in my home lab. Every project I complete is a step toward building the skills, the portfolio, and the credibility to eventually work for myself and nobody else.

07 — Speculate on the Future

Challenges, Opportunities, and Jobs That Do Not Exist Yet

No plan survives contact with reality unchanged. I am building toward something unconventional, and I am clear-eyed about what that means.

Challenges to Overcome

Running a solo design-and-build operation requires skills across engineering, fabrication, business development, and client communication. Finding clients in a niche market is harder than finding a job at an established firm. The financial risk of self-employment, especially early on, is real. I expect to spend several years in industry building capital, skills, and connections before the studio is viable on its own.

Opportunities Emerging Now

Affordable brushless servo systems, open-source motion control firmware, and low-cost 3D printing have dramatically lowered the barrier to building sophisticated animatronics. The immersive entertainment market, including escape rooms, haunted attractions, and location-based VR, is growing fast and hungry for creative hardware talent.

A Job That Does Not Exist Yet: AI-Augmented Animatronics Designer

Within the next decade, machine learning will likely handle real-time motion refinement and behavioral responsiveness in animatronic systems. A new kind of role will emerge: someone who designs the physical form and mechanical architecture, while AI handles the fine-grained motion adaptation that makes a character feel truly alive. That is a role I want to help define. The engineering foundation I am building now is exactly the right starting point for it.

08 — Conclusion

Engineering Is How I Say Things

The future I am designing toward is one where I make my living creating things that move, inspire, and surprise. That goal is not separate from who I already am. It is the logical endpoint of a decade of building, tinkering, and iterating in my home lab and in the field.

ISTA 100 gave me a framework to think more deliberately about design as a process rather than a result. The habits it reinforced, understanding the user, iterating on problems, communicating ideas visually, are ones I will carry into every project and every job I take.

Dreams are designs. They get better with every iteration. This one is just getting started.
